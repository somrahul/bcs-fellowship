Section 3B: Mentoring & Coaching

Evidentiary record of fostering technical independence, AI safety judgment, and systems thinking across industry teams and university graduate programs.

Application Statement (STAR Technique • 379 Words)

Situation

Building enterprise-grade artificial intelligence and connected systems demands that practitioners develop specialized architectural judgment. Engineers, product managers, and researchers cannot rely on routine templates; they must actively reason through model variance, inference latency, data freshness, and user trust under rigorous production constraints. Across my leadership tenures at Walmart Global Tech and Lutron Electronics, and through academic appointments, I observed that teams often struggled to transition from task-level execution to system-level architectural ownership. Passive supervision does not develop technical leaders.

Task

I took on direct responsibility for mentoring and coaching product managers, UX researchers, designers, and software engineers across Walmart and Lutron, while teaching graduate students at the University of Pennsylvania (UPenn) and Lehigh University. My objective was clear: cultivate technical independence, rigorous systems thinking, and end-to-end operational ownership so that practitioners could independently architect and lead complex AI and IoT initiatives.

Action

I structured my mentorship around structured 1:1 coaching, architecture reviews, and production post-mortems:

  • Architectural Judgment & AI Safety: Rather than prescribing answers, I coached mentees to evaluate model failure modes, data leakage risks, and inference costs upfront. I trained them to treat latency budgets, privacy safeguards, and bias mitigation as non-negotiable architectural requirements.
  • Production Readiness & Operational Ownership: On mission-critical AI audio and computer vision initiatives, I guided engineers and PMs to author comprehensive operational runbooks, automated regression suites, and real-time observability dashboards prior to launch.
  • Root-Cause Incident Retrospectives: When production anomalies occurred, I coached teams through blameless root-cause analysis, teaching them to distinguish transient model variance from systemic algorithmic drift.
  • Academic Masterclasses & Curriculum: At UPenn, I designed and delivered graduate masterclasses in Integrated Product Design on 'The AI Product Orchestrator' and 'The Evolution of Product Roles in the Age of AI.' At Lehigh University, I delivered guest lectures on ambient computing and Zero UI architectures.
Result

My coaching directly enabled multiple mentees to secure promotions into senior and lead product management and engineering roles across Walmart and Lutron. Mentees who initially required guidance on baseline feature specifications now independently govern multi-million-dollar AI platforms and lead executive technical reviews. Across UPenn, Lehigh, and IEEE STEM outreach programs, I have coached over 100 graduate students and emerging engineers, equipping them with the architectural rigor required to lead in the global computing industry.
