Section 3B · Professional impact — Mentoring and coachingStatement as submitted · 429 words
Situation

Shipping AI to customers asks something of engineers and product managers that ordinary feature work doesn't. A model's output varies run to run. Latency budgets are real money. The failures that matter turn up in production rather than in a demo. Across Walmart, Lutron and two universities I saw the same pattern. Capable people who could execute a well-specified component would stall the moment they had to own a system end to end. That isn't a skills gap. It's a judgment gap, and judgment doesn't transfer through documentation.

Task

I took responsibility for closing it, and not informally. At Lutron I led the design and research team. I ran their performance reviews and wrote their promotion cases. At Walmart I lead the cross-functional groups that ship these platforms, engineers, data scientists, designers and analysts, and I coach them through the calls they have to own themselves. I also teach graduate students at Pennsylvania and Lehigh.

Action

The coaching happens in four places: Formal review, not encouragement: at Lutron I wrote and signed the annual reviews and the promotion cases for my team, and I sat on the hiring panel for the design and product organisation. Deciding who comes in and who moves up is where mentoring stops being advice. Making them argue it: I don't hand people answers. In design review I make a mentee argue for the alternative they rejected and name the failure mode they're most worried about. If they can't say what being wrong would cost, they aren't ready to sign off. Ownership before launch: on the Shop-the-background and AI audio work I won't approve a release until the runbook and regression suite exist and someone's name is on the observability dashboard. Ownership is a precondition, not a follow-up ticket. Teaching: at UPenn I built and delivered graduate masterclasses in Integrated Product Design, and at Lehigh I lectured on ambient computing and Zero UI architectures.

Result

Shop the Background alone took more than thirty-five people across eight functions, and I led that group without a reporting line into any of them. Three of the people I coached there were promoted on my recommendation: Yash Garg, Abhimanyu Chadha and Daniel Lee, each now owns a larger scope than the one I coached them through. At Lutron several of the people whose cases I wrote went on to mentor others and lead programmes of their own, that's the outcome I care about most. Add the graduate students and early-career engineers I've taught at Pennsylvania, Lehigh and IEEE outreach, and the number of people I've coached or taught is past a hundred.
