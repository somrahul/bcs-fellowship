Section 3A · Body of work — ResponsibilityStatement as submitted · 440 words
Situation

Walmart serves 255 million shoppers a week. When I took over Emerging Technology, immersive computing wasn't a platform, it was five engineering organizations each running their own pilots. Every team had its own asset format and its own idea of what finished meant. Nobody owned cross-platform performance. Latency over cellular was costing us conversions, and features shipped without a name on the sign-off. That second one is the real problem. You can fix a slow renderer. You can't fix an organization where nobody is accountable for what ships.

Task

As Staff Product Manager for Emerging Technology I own the architecture strategy, engineering standards and production sign-off for Walmart's immersive computing platforms. That spans the generative multimodal pipelines, the real-time spatial rendering underneath them and the clients they serve, across five partner organizations in engineering, computer vision, data science and infrastructure. The sign-off is the part that matters, because it's where shared accountability stops and one name goes on the release. That name is mine.

Action

Four things had to change, and I owned all four:

Standards, and enforcing them: I set platform SLAs binding on every partner team, asset streaming under 2.0 seconds over cellular, 60 FPS in the viewport, automated regression on release. I've held launches with executive visibility when a latency or fidelity budget was breached. A standard nobody enforces isn't a standard.

Settling the arguments: Five organizations meant five roadmaps. When inference latency, rendering fidelity, compute budget and ship date pulled against each other, I made the call rather than escalating it, and took the contested ones to executive review myself.

Products, not demos: View in Home: Paint puts a real paint color on a customer's own wall, in their own light. Beauty and Optical Virtual Try-On, Shop the Background and AI Audio shipped on the same platform. Getting one of these to a demo takes a weekend. Getting four to production is the job.

Standards that outlive me: I set unified 3D schemas, automated geometric validation and shared asset contracts. Partner teams build against them by default, so one-off pilot integrations became a platform others extend without asking me.

Result

The standards I set now govern immersive commerce across Walmart globally. View in Home: Paint lifted conversion 300 to 600 basis points, Beauty Virtual Try-On raised Gen Z engagement 10%, and the platform carries more than 165,000 AR-enabled items. These platforms generate over $100 million in annual enterprise revenue and over $140 million in gross merchandise impact. Walmart's Make A Difference Award and a top 1% rating followed. The number I'd point at isn't the revenue, it's the 165,000 items, because that's the one that says the platform holds.
